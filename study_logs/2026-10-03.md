## Key corrections

### 1. Going to a drinking party
- Original: `I did go to drinking party.`
- Better: `I went to a drinking party.`
- More natural: `I went out for drinks with some coworkers.`

### 2. Being in charge of finance
- Original: `She account for the financial for our team.`
- Better: `She's in charge of finance for our team.`

### 3. First impression
- Original idea: `I guess she is very majime.`
- Better: `I thought she was pretty serious.`
- Useful pattern: `Before I met her, I thought she was pretty serious.`

### 4. Impression changed
- Original: `After drinking party it was changed.`
- Better: `I thought she was serious, but she was really friendly.`
- Alternative: `She was completely different from what I expected.`

### 5. Talking about drinking
- Avoid: `She is very alcoholic.`
- Better: `She really likes drinking.`
- If emphasizing tolerance: `She can really hold her liquor.`

### 6. Friendly / easy to talk to
- `frank` is understandable, but for this context:
- Better: `She's really friendly and easy to talk to.`
- Useful phrase: `She made me laugh a lot.`

### 7. Food
- Original: `I not interested in the foods.`
- Better: `Honestly, I wasn't that interested in the food.`
- More natural for the intended meaning: `I enjoyed talking with everyone more than the food.`

### 8. Disneyland plan
- Original: `We made the promise for going to Disneyland.`
- Better: `We made plans to go to Disneyland together.`
- Simple: `We planned to go to Disneyland.`

### 9. Asking how someone is doing
- Original: `I want to know how do he do in these days.`
- Better: `I'm curious how Sekiguchi-san is doing.`
- Better for recent condition: `I want to know how he's been doing lately.`

### 10. Business trip
- Better sentence: `I'm going to the U.S. from November 2nd to November 9th, so I need to practice English conversation before then.`

### 11. Company relationship with Google
- Avoid: `My company ties up with Google.`
- Better: `My company works with Google.`
- Alternative: `My company has a partnership with Google.`

### 12. Training / 研修
- `研修` → `training`
- Useful sentence: `I'm going to the U.S. on a business trip for training at Google Cloud in San Francisco.`

## Practice focus

- Prioritize simple, natural expressions over literal Japanese-to-English translation.
- Review recurring issues: articles (`a`), prepositions (`on a business trip`), and past tense (`thought`, `was`).
